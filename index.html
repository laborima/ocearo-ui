Built for sailors

Everything you need to know, at a glance.

Chartplotters show lines on a map. Ocearo shows what is really happening around your boat, the way a self-driving car shows the road: your boat, the wind, the sea state, the other vessels and what each of them must do.

The 3D view

Your boat, the sea and the seabed, live.

A real-time 3D scene centred on your boat, built from your own instruments. One button switches between five views of the world around it.

  • A sea built from the true wind. Wind sea and swell, whitecaps from 7 knots, the sun where it really is and your own wake.
  • Live chart with buoyage. OpenStreetMap and OpenSeaMap lights and marks, true to scale with the AIS targets.
  • The seabed in 3D. SHOM 5–20 m surveys on the French coast, soundings at the current tide, shallow water against your draft hatched in orange.

Traffic and the rules of the road

Who gives way? Ocearo tells you.

Every AIS target is drawn as its real ship type and size, glides between reports, and is coloured by who must keep clear — with the rule and the alteration to make.

  • Rules 12 to 18 applied live, from each target’s AIS status and type and from your own: under sail, a yacht is not the same vessel as under engine.
  • Clear advice: “Avoid: 15° to port”, the closest point of approach on a dashed line, and a warning when no single alteration clears everyone.
  • Spoken watch with Ocearo Core: “Collision danger: LE PERTUIS at 0.5 miles… pass astern of her.”

Tactics and weather

Sail faster, trim better.

Ocearo draws your sails from the apparent wind and shows where your polars will take you. Racing or cruising, you see the next move.

  • Sails you can trim from the screen: draft stripes, telltales, sheet cars, reefs and the asymmetric spinnaker.
  • Laylines and isochrones: where you will be in 5, 10 and 15 minutes on every heading, VMG advice and a polar ghost boat.
  • 48-hour wind forecast around the boat in Windy colours, kept on board for offline use.

Safety

When it matters most, one button.

The moments you train for and hope never happen. Ocearo keeps them simple, visible and loud.

  • Man overboard: drop point, the person’s estimated position now from current and leeway, the drift line and the bearing to steer.
  • Anchor watch: alarm radius, the rode and the real swing track — a dragging anchor shows long before the alarm.
  • Shallow water and parking assist: depth against your draft, and the predicted track into your berth with wind and current.

Ocean Robot

Today an extra pair of eyes. Tomorrow, the pilot.

Ocearo stands for Ocean Robot. Like a self-driving car learns to see the road, Ocearo is learning to see the sea — and, step by step, to sail with you.

  1. Today

    An aid to the watch

    A live picture of the boat and the traffic, the rules of the road worked out for every target, spoken alerts, anchor and man-overboard watch.

  2. Next

    Seeing with cameras

    Ocearo Vision, in development, uses on-board cameras to spot what AIS does not show — small boats, buoys, floating obstacles — to read the sails’ trim and to guide you into the berth.

  3. The goal

    Autopilot by vision

    A pilot that keeps the watch, applies the rules of the road and steers through the Signal K autopilot — with the skipper always in command.

Get started

Up and running in five minutes.

Ocearo is a Signal K webapp. If your boat already runs Signal K, you are one click away.

  1. Install

    Signal K server ≥ 2: Appstore → Available → ocearo-ui, or from the command line.

    npm install ocearo-ui

  2. Open

    Restart the server and open Ocearo on any screen on board: chart table, tablet or phone.

    http://<signalk-server>:3000/ocearo-ui/

  3. Go further

    Add Ocearo Core for the logbook, anchor watch, SHOM bathymetry, spoken alerts and the AI copilot.

    npm install ocearo-core

FAQ

Questions sailors ask

Is Ocearo open source?

Yes, under the Apache 2.0 licence. The code of Ocearo UI and Ocearo Core is on GitHub: you can read it, adapt it to your boat and contribute.

What hardware do I need?

A Signal K server, which a Raspberry Pi 4 or 5 runs comfortably, connected to your instruments (NMEA 2000 or 0183). Any recent browser on a tablet, phone or chart-table screen is a display.

Does it work without internet?

Yes. Your instruments, AIS and the 3D view need no internet. Chart tiles, wind forecasts and bathymetry are kept on board whenever you have a connection, and can be downloaded for the area before you leave.

Is it a certified navigation system?

No. Ocearo is an aid to the watch, like a second pair of eyes. Keep a proper lookout and use official charts; the skipper stays responsible for the boat.

Which boats does it support?

Any boat that publishes standard Signal K data: sailing yachts, catamarans and motor boats. The 3D view offers a 10.8 m racer, a 14 m catamaran and simpler models.

Which languages are available?

Twelve: English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Dutch, Portuguese, Danish, Swedish, Finnish, Polish and Greek, with metric, imperial or nautical units.

How is Ocearo built?

Mostly with AI coding assistants, in the interest of transparency. The author, a developer and sailor, decides what the app does, reviews the changes and tests it on board, on the boat it is developed on. Code reviews and bug reports on GitHub are especially welcome.
